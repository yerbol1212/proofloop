## First owned phone result

The [28 October phone report](../artifacts/feasibility/phone-session-20261028.md) records one 47.4-second clip, eight selected original frames, one live Spark compilation and 16 live SAM image calls. The checker returned **pass → fail → unknown → pass**. The bounded planner proposed moving the fork from RIGHT to LEFT and inspecting it afterward. Its prediction never wrote measured facts or cleared the wrong-state result; fresh post-correction observations supplied the last pass.

Technical timestamp selection, calibration and exact-contract review were performed by the assistant under the user's direct instruction to proceed. The report explicitly records that a person did not review the exact contract or selected offsets. Region names follow the handwritten paper labels: they correspond to upper/lower bands in the displayed portrait video. Calibration and the contract were frozen before SAM inference.

The estimated cost for this round is $0.045654 at the published Standard rates. SAM median latency was 1.2425 seconds and Spark took 4.987 seconds. The report preserves the one-clip, declared-unique-target and selected-frame limits. It proves neither complete visibility nor exact counts; it is not an interactive repair or held-out accuracy result. Original media, extracted images, raw responses and private authorization text remain local.

## Added workflow and online evaluation

The workbench's task editor supports one to six unique target classes in two reviewed named regions, such as DISPENSE and STAGING. Custom drafts compile explicit placement requirements through Spark; the fixed coffee preset creates an authored unapproved draft without a compilation request. Drafting does not change the active contract or verdict. Exact reviewed activation binds the requirements, checks, concepts, perception mode and guided method version, then invalidates earlier evidence. Older guided versions cannot silently inherit that review. The [live task-editor record](../artifacts/feasibility/task-editor-live-20261028.json) captures one real Spark compilation with swapped fork/spoon requirements and assistant-authorized activation; it claims neither human review nor physical execution.

Unknown evidence now produces a typed inspection plan from validated locator fields, source-bound SAM measurements and checker observations. Requests name the affected object and view and distinguish visibility, identity, score, region support, stability and freshness. Generated explanation text does not select the policy branch. The server counts follow-up submissions before model dispatch, including failed model attempts, preserves the budget through unresolved new inspections and stops after two follow-ups. Reused sources cannot satisfy a new request; confidence thresholds remain unchanged. A missing detection cannot establish absence or an unseen physical mistake. An optional Spark-box-guided SAM mode receives object concepts without desired placements or acceptance regions; Spark assigns identity and SAM refines its proposed boundary. The mask is not independent semantic-identity verification.

The [historical online report](../artifacts/feasibility/online-video-20261028.md) preserves the original eight-case pilot (**text SAM 0/8**, all UNKNOWN; **Spark 7/8**) and guided v1's four-case comparison on episodes 000004/000005 (**guided 2/4; text SAM 0/4; Spark 4/4**).

The [guided-fix report](../artifacts/feasibility/guided-fix-20261028.md) records v2's normalized 0–1000 coordinate contract and conservative conversion to source pixels. Identity ambiguity, full target visibility and ordinary boundary imprecision are separate flags. Uncertain identity or incomplete visibility stops that target's SAM request; approximate boundaries may be refined by SAM when identity and visibility are supported. Confidence thresholds remain unchanged, and a refined mask still cannot independently establish Spark's semantic identity.

V2 matched **4/4 on a development replay** of the already tested episodes 000004/000005. It then matched **3/4 on four fresh source pairs** from episodes 000006/000007, with one UNKNOWN, versus text SAM **0/4** and Spark **4/4**. The unresolved dispense case has partial first-view occlusion and no first-view SAM result; the second-view SAM score is **0.746**, below the approved **0.8** threshold. It correctly remains unverified rather than receiving a weaker threshold.

The earlier placement cohorts contain **eight original coffee recordings and 16 unique placement pairs**. That v2 placement comparison evaluated four development replay pairs plus four fresh pairs; replay does not add independent source cases. These small, distinct cohorts do not show a complete fix or better acceptance accuracy than Spark. Original failures and development exposure remain disclosed.

The Video lab exposes these results, source/license attribution, selected images, zones and explanations without new model calls. Source media remains local. Early staging frames are normal intermediate demonstration states that fail an authored placement gate; they are not publisher-labeled robot mistakes. Cases share a task/camera and are correlated. These results establish neither natural-error recognition, continuous action recognition, physical closed-loop recovery, held-out reliability nor validated market demand. See [sources](ONLINE-VIDEO-SOURCES.md) and the [benchmark procedure](BENCHMARK.md).

## Bounded follow-up, task transfer and preparation

The [fixed future-evidence evaluation](../artifacts/feasibility/fixed-followup-20261028.md) selected all six pairs before inference. Its initial inconsistent windows need two new, consistent views; later pairs are strictly after the consumed source time, have distinct hashes and are at least one source second apart. Guided matched **4/6** labels, Spark **6/6**. In chronological replay, guided resolved episode 000000 only on its second follow-up, while episode 000001 remained UNKNOWN when its source ended. Spark resolved both on the first follow-up. A deliberately terminal third trajectory remained UNKNOWN for both.

The replay consumes no additional model requests. All-pair inference used **17 real requests**, estimated **$0.119590**. Spark's unnecessary second future pair for its already resolved first trajectory is explicitly separated as one counterfactual request; it is excluded from policy-required work. All three source episodes were already exposed in development, so this adds **zero independent coffee recordings**. The six pairs have three UNKNOWN and three PASS labels, with no FAIL control. No viewpoint, lighting or robot action was caused by the policy. [Machine-readable results](../artifacts/feasibility/online-fixed-followup-20261028.json).

The [candy transfer check](NEW-TASK-TRANSFER-20261028.md) adds **one** original recording from another ALOHA manipulation task and scene under a separately reviewed MIT-declared dataset card. Guided and Spark both matched **2/2** authored labels: early UNKNOWN and final PASS. The early guided locator supplied packet/label-area anchors, which the unchanged SAM confidence gate rejected. This is an important semantic-identity limitation, not proof that the distractor was correctly identified. No thresholds, prompts, timestamps or regions were tuned after the run. Its eight actual requests cost an estimated **$0.048412**. There is no FAIL case or broad generalization claim. [Public result](../artifacts/feasibility/online-candy-task-transfer-20261028.json).

The authored [placement preparation workflow](WORKFLOW.md) requires exact task review, current supported PASS and replay of saved evidence before a separate operator declaration to continue. Historical video, UNKNOWN, FAIL, expired evidence, missing replay or an inspection error blocks continuation. The server rechecks saved files and freshness when recording the declaration, so an earlier successful replay cannot grant stale permission. The record is explicitly `operator_declared_continuation_not_observed`; neither a button click nor a PASS proves dispensing or a complete procedure.

A [public reproduction CLI](REPRODUCING.md) now prints a plan by default, with no network or model access. An explicitly authorized preparation downloads only pinned source bytes, preserves the dataset card/attribution/MIT notice, records actual decoded frame timestamps and produces an unapproved contract. The one-recording, four-frame preparation was executed in an isolated fresh source checkout with zero API calls. Human visual review and optional paid Meta inference remain separate steps. Another person's ability to follow the guide and the practical value of this workflow have not yet been tested.

## Remaining validation gates

- A current operator follows a displayed inspection/repair request and supplies genuinely new physical evidence; prerecorded outcomes cannot close this gate.
- Independent target identity under semantic distractors, more diverse cameras/lighting and wrong-placement FAIL controls for the new task remain unresolved.
- The preparation checkpoint covers reviewed image-zone placement. Hidden state, event history, dispensing, quality, safety and full-procedure completion require separately validated observation methods.
- A prospective user or judge reproduces the public-clone flow and evaluates whether the bounded preparation workflow solves a practical need.

Separate injected-client fixtures exercised fail, pass, unknown, request errors and disconnection; these made zero Meta calls and establish UI/software behavior only. These are authored software diagnostics, not model accuracy or physical benchmark scores. The raw [oracle report](../artifacts/feasibility/oracle-report.md) preserves provenance and per-case outcomes.

The Spark compiler uses a strict schema and local semantic validation. Two initial real Spark compilations produced the requested object/region counts. Swapping the requested regions changed the protocol checks and content hash. Those two initial drafts use count predicates and require human approval.

The SAM-to-checker bridge accepts decoded masks with source hashes, approved region rectangles and explicit evidence origin. It permits positive visibility and count lower bounds, requires distinct sources for repeated evidence, and never automatically sets an absence-validation contract. Consequently exact-count passes remain unknown unless independently supported. This is a deliberate limitation of the current conservative observation path.

The separate authored visibility protocol passed on two distinct generated source hashes. A screwdriver in the wrong region was grounded there, while unproven absence stayed unknown. The occluded screwdriver also stayed unknown. The compiler's exact-count drafts have not passed through this observation path; the completeness contract remains an implementation gate.

The added `location` predicate checks positive placement under an explicit, operator-confirmed unique-target scene contract. Its compiler mode matched the supplied synthetic requirements in a [real Spark call](../artifacts/feasibility/live-location-20261026.json). Software tests exercise stable wrong placement → fail, missing or multiple targets → unknown, and fresh corrected placement → pass. These tests do not establish physical perception or infer scene uniqueness. The exact-count limitation remains unchanged.

The [decoder experiment](../artifacts/feasibility/capture-decoder-20261026.md) validates real extraction from generated video, including a true display-rotation matrix and off-grid frame requests. The [session diagnostic](../artifacts/feasibility/session-oracle-20261026.json) exercises the recorded observation loop with authored SAM-shaped results. Both are explicitly synthetic, and the session diagnostic makes zero model calls. The [session guide](SESSIONS.md) documents approval and inference boundaries.

The participant's available objects are a fork and spoon. The [cutlery concept probe](../artifacts/feasibility/cutlery-concepts-20261027.md) used real SAM on one generated image and returned the intended positive detections. This adds an estimated $0.005 in image calls and does not validate the participant's physical cutlery. The old tool scenes remain historical evidence; the current [capture guide](CAPTURE.md) and [starter](../examples/cutlery/README.md) use the available objects.
